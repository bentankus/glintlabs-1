## Evidence for why action taking matters

Use these data points when explaining why taking action matters:

- People who do not believe action will be taken are **7x more likely to report being disengaged** than those who believe action will be taken.
- Teams where managers used Viva Glint action planning showed **7% increased scores on average** relative to company changes in the areas where the manager took action within a quarter.
- Teams high on action planning are more likely to report confidence in leadership and excitement about the company's future prospects.
- A single quarter can be enough for meaningful score increases when managers take action.
- Taking action in any area can increase confidence in leadership, indirectly affecting engagement and performance.

## Core shift

The document reframes action planning as **action taking**:

- favor small, visible, iterative steps over large static plans
- make action collaborative between manager and team
- embed action into normal team rhythms
- use feedback as a learning opportunity, not a diagnosis of failure

## Why action taking fails

Manager guidance should help avoid common failure modes:

- ownership is unclear or action is treated as HR's responsibility
- managers feel responsible for fixing everything
- conversations about feedback feel personal or intimidating
- managers focus on elaborate plans instead of immediate action
- teams lose momentum after initial results sharing
- support and enablement for managers is limited
- managers do not control every systemic driver

## People Success principles

| Principle | Meaning for the skill |
|---|---|
| Simple | Avoid long catalogs and over-engineered plans. Help the manager focus on a few high-impact opportunities. |
| Collaborative | Prefer actions that involve team discussion, shared interpretation, and joint ownership. |
| Integrated | Prefer actions that can fit into existing meetings, goals, learning, and business routines. |

## ACT conversation framework

Use ACT when helping a manager facilitate a team conversation around survey results.

| Step | Meaning | Manager prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledge where we are | Share results, discuss the current state, and create shared understanding. | "What is the feedback telling us?" |
| Collaborate on where we want to go | Identify priorities, desired future state, and what matters most to the team. | "Which issue would make the biggest difference if improved?" |
| Take one step forward together | Choose one small next step and define shared accountability. | "What is one thing we can try before our next check-in?" |

ACT works because it:

- creates trust by openly discussing feedback with the team
- gets to root behaviors by asking what to start, stop, and continue
- creates individual commitment and shared accountability
- encourages learning and iteration rather than defensiveness
- makes action a repeatable habit

## Three habits

Action taking should reinforce three repeating habits:

1. **Conversations** - discuss feedback, choose focus areas, check progress, and refine actions.
2. **Goals** - turn the chosen focus area into a clear, visible, shared goal.
3. **Learning** - use resources, experiments, and skill-building to help the team improve.

## Manager roles

When framing recommendations, consider which manager role the selected action record asks the manager to play:

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Coach | Show confidence in the team, keep focus, model candor/vulnerability/accountability, and recognize desired behaviors. |
| Facilitator | Help the team reach an outcome, seek differing opinions, and ensure all voices are heard. |
| Roadblock remover | Escalate and navigate constraints such as policies, work structures, budget, or cross-team dependencies. |
| Connector | Keep meaningful conversations going through team meetings, 1:1s, and links to other groups or leaders. |

## Small shift examples

Use these as examples of "take one step" framing:

- Recognition focus: begin regular meetings by having team members recognize someone else's support.
- Empowerment focus: ask the team for the top three things they want the manager to delegate or step out of.
- Risk-taking focus: ask team members weekly whether they tried something different and what happened.
- Downward communication focus: send three bullets after each leadership meeting with key points for the team.

## Selection implications

Use this as a secondary ranking lens when choosing among authorized taxonomy action records:

1. Prioritize impact over quantity. Do not recommend many actions at once.
2. Tie recommendations to team goals and business priorities.
3. Prefer actions that start a manager-team conversation.
4. Favor small actions that can begin immediately.
5. Prefer actions that create an ongoing check-in rhythm.
6. Favor collaborative ownership over manager-only activity.
7. Use a growth-mindset frame: learn, experiment, iterate, and improve.
8. Prefer actions that can fit into an existing meeting or workflow over actions requiring a separate new meeting.
9. Consider the manager's role: coach, facilitator, roadblock remover, or connector.

## Output implications

When using this context, the skill should:

- recommend one best-fit action when evidence is clear
- otherwise present 2-3 eligible candidates and ask the manager/team to choose
- include an ACT-style conversation guide when the user wants manager-facing guidance
- end with one concrete next step and one follow-up/check-in moment
- suggest 30 minutes in an existing meeting for initial results discussion and 10-15 minute follow-ups for progress
- avoid implying that the manager must complete every action record in a candidate set
