## Psychological safety definition

Psychological safety means the team has a shared understanding that people can take risks, share thoughts and worries, ask questions, and acknowledge errors without fear of embarrassment, belittling, rejection, or punishment.

In manager-facing language:

> Team members feel safe to speak up, ask for help, raise concerns, and admit mistakes without fear of negative consequences.

## Three manager actions

### 1. Check negative reactivity

Managers should notice and regulate defensive or negative reactions such as irritation, feeling insulted, anger, or the impulse to shut someone down by explaining why they are wrong.

Manager behaviors:

- build self-awareness
- pause when activated
- ask to circle back when needed
- resist taking feedback personally
- replace blame with curiosity

### 2. Listen without agenda

Listening without agenda means focusing on understanding and empathy rather than immediately solving, explaining, or controlling the conversation.

Manager behaviors:

- listen to understand, not just to gather information
- show curiosity and openness
- avoid judgment
- demonstrate that employee perspectives are valued

### 3. Model the vulnerability you hope to see

Managers build trust by admitting mistakes, normalizing learning, and showing that it is safe not to be perfect.

Manager behaviors:

- admit and normalize mistakes
- share what was learned
- show how learning affects future decisions
- frame mistakes as part of growth

## Selection implications

Use this as a secondary ranking lens when choosing among authorized taxonomy records:

1. For voice/speaking-up questions, prefer action records that create trust and make it safer to raise concerns.
2. Prefer manager behaviors that are visible in team interactions: pausing reactivity, listening with curiosity, and modeling fallibility.
3. Prefer team conversation records when the goal is to increase voice or psychological safety.
4. Prefer learning-resource records when the user asks for education, enablement, or manager development.
5. Do not use this context to recommend a psychological safety action for an unrelated question unless the taxonomy authorizes it.

## ACT framing

When adding ACT framing:

- **Acknowledge:** name that speaking up may not feel safe or easy today.
- **Collaborate:** ask what would make it safer to ask questions, raise concerns, or admit mistakes.
- **Take one step:** choose one manager behavior to practice before the next check-in, such as pausing before responding defensively or asking one curiosity-based follow-up question.
